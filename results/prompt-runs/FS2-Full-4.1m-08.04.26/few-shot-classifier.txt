### **0-Limitation of Liability:** Covers clauses excluding or restricting a seller's legal liability for non-performance or injury, which is unfair when it creates a significant imbalance by stripping consumers of compensation rights for death or gross negligence, but remains fair if it is transparent, proportional, and explicitly protects mandatory statutory rights.

### **1-Unilateral Termination:** Involves terms allowing a seller to dissolve the contract at will, which is considered unfair when it lacks reasonable notice or reciprocal rights for the consumer, but can be fair if the contract specifies objective grounds for termination and provides a sufficient notice period.

### **2-Unilateral Change:** Identifies provisions enabling the seller to alter contract terms or prices without consent, which is unfair if it lacks a valid, pre-specified reason, but is considered fair if the seller provides advance notice, a valid justification, and the right for the consumer to terminate the contract immediately without penalty.  

### **3-Content Removal:** Identifies clauses that grant the provider the right to delete, hide, or restrict access to content uploaded by the user.  Such terms are classified as unfair when they allow for arbitrary removal without a specified valid reason or notice, as this creates a significant imbalance in the rights of the parties. To be considered fair, the term must be transparent and specify objective grounds for removal, ensuring the consumer is provided with a clear legal rationale for the provider's actions and accompanied by a notification and appeal process.

### **4-Contract by Using:** Identifies 'browse-wrap' provisions where the mere use is deemed acceptance of terms, which is unfair under Directive 93/13/EEC as it creates a significant imbalance by binding consumers without informed consent or a genuine opportunity to review the obligations, but it becomes fair when acceptance is conditioned on a clear, affirmative action—like a 'click-wrap' agreement—performed after the consumer has been presented with the terms in a transparent manner. If a clause gives a clear right to reject changes by stopping use or terminating without penalty, prefer [8] over [4].

### **5-Choice of Law:** Designates the legal system governing the contract, which is unfair if it selects a foreign law to deprive consumers of the mandatory protections of their home country, but is fair if it explicitly preserves the consumer's right to their local mandatory legal safeguards.

### **6-Jurisdiction:** Specifies the court location for disputes, which is unfair if it forces consumers to litigate in a distant or inconvenient forum thereby hindering access to justice, but is fair if it allows the consumer to bring legal proceedings in the courts of their own domicile.

### **7-Arbitration:** Requires disputes to be settled via private arbitration, which is unfair if it is mandatory and binding—thereby hindering the consumer's right to legal action in court—but is fair if it is an optional, low-cost, and non-binding alternative that does not preclude judicial remedies. If users can opt out or arbitration is not mandatory, prefer [8] over [7].

### **8-Fair:** This category is a catch-all for any clause that is balanced, transparent, and does not meet the criteria for any of the unfair categories (0–7). Use this label only if no unfair elements are present.
