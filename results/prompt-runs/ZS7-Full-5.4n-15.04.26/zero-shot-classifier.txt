## LEGAL CATEGORIES & CRITERIA
### **0.Limitation of Liability**
- Definition: Provider liability to user is limited
- Unfair: Excludes or restricts provider liability for non-performance, personal injury, death, or gross negligence  when it creates a significant imbalance by stripping consumers of compensation rights.
- Fair: Clauses that are transparent, proportional, and explicitly protect the consumer's mandatory statutory rights.
- Note: Do not assign [0] for warranty language (for example, "as is" or "no warranty") or third-party resource disclaimers unless the clause also expressly excludes or caps the provider's liability for user damages, losses, or compensation."

### **1.Unilateral Termination**
    Definition: Terms allowing the provider to dissolve the contract at will.
    Unfair: Termination with no notice or insufficient notice or where reciprocal rights are not provided for the consumer, even if the user has violated the agreement.
    Fair: Specifying objective grounds for termination and providing a sufficient notice period."

### **2.Unilateral Change**
    Unfair: Altering terms or prices without a valid, pre-specified reason or the consumer's consent.
    Fair: Providing advance notice, valid justification, and the right for the consumer to terminate the contract immediately without penalty."

### **3.Content Removal**
    Unfair: Granting the provider arbitrary rights to delete or hide user content without a clear reason or notice.
    Fair: Specifying objective grounds for removal and offering a transparent notification and appeal process."

### **4-Contract by Using:** Identifies 'browse-wrap' provisions where the mere use is deemed acceptance of terms, which is unfair under Directive 93/13/EEC as it creates a significant imbalance by binding consumers without informed consent or a genuine opportunity to review the obligations, but it becomes fair when acceptance is conditioned on a clear, affirmative action—like a 'click-wrap' agreement—performed after the consumer has been presented with the terms in a transparent manner.

### **5-Choice of Law:** Designates the legal system governing the contract, which is unfair if it selects a foreign law to deprive consumers of the mandatory protections of their home country, but is fair if it explicitly preserves the consumer's right to their local mandatory legal safeguards.

### **6-Jurisdiction:** Specifies the court location for disputes, which is unfair if it forces consumers to litigate in a distant or inconvenient forum thereby hindering access to justice, but is fair if it allows the consumer to bring legal proceedings in the courts of their own domicile.

### **7-Arbitration:** Requires disputes to be settled via private arbitration, which is unfair if it is mandatory and binding—thereby hindering the consumer's right to legal action in court—but is fair if it is an optional, low-cost, and non-binding alternative that does not preclude judicial remedies. If users can opt out or arbitration is not mandatory, prefer [8] over [7]. Also, arbitration procedural details alone (administrator, rules, venue mechanics, fee allocation, or FAA-governance wording) are [8] unless the clause imposes mandatory binding arbitration as the dispute forum.

### **8.Fair:** This category is a catch-all for any clause that is balanced, transparent, and does not meet the criteria for any of the unfair categories (0–7). Use this label only if no unfair elements are present.
